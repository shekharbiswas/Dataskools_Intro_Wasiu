# 📘 Day 5 — Concepts: Canva & Presentation Guidelines

## 🎯 Goal for Today
Wrap up the week by turning our pipeline work into a short, clean
presentation — the skill of explaining our work to someone else in a
few slides, not a document.

---

## 🎨 Canva Basics (Guidelines, Not Rules)

- **Start from a template** — pick a simple "Presentation" template in
  Canva rather than a blank canvas; it keeps spacing and fonts consistent
  without extra effort.
- **One idea per slide** — if a slide needs two headlines, it's two slides.
- **Keep text short** — bullet points, not paragraphs. If we're reading
  off the slide, it's too much text.
- **Stick to 2–3 colors** — reuse the same palette across every slide
  (Canva templates usually already do this — don't fight it).
- **Use one font pairing** — one font for headings, one for body text.
  Canva templates handle this by default; avoid adding a third.
- **Leave whitespace** — a slide with empty space looks intentional; a
  slide packed edge-to-edge looks rushed.

---

## 🗣️ Presentation Guidelines

- **Open with what the pipeline does** — one sentence, before any detail.
- **Follow the pipeline's actual flow** — source → clean → document →
  reflect, in that order, matching the order we actually did the work.
- **Show, don't just tell** — reuse the diagram from Day 2 rather than
  re-describing it in words.
- **End with one takeaway** — what we'd do differently next time, or what
  we're proudest of (straight from our Day 3 journal entry).
- **Aim for 5 slides, no more than 7** — short enough to present in
  under 3 minutes.

---

## ❓ One Reflection Prompt
If someone outside this course watched our presentation, would they
understand what a "data pipeline" is by the end of it? That's the real
test for today.
