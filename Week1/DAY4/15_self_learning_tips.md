# 📚 Self-learning tips: growing your confidence and curiosity

No one can learn for you — but you’re never truly learning alone either.

Every learner’s path is unique. Some move quickly, others take it step by step.  
Some feel confident early, others build confidence over time. All of that is okay.

In this course, we give you structure.  
Your mentors give you guidance.  
But the **real transformation** begins when **you take charge of your learning** — when you start choosing curiosity over fear, progress over perfection.

You don’t need to get everything right.  
You just need to keep showing up, asking questions, and building small wins each week.

Let’s talk about how to grow that spark 🔥

<br>

## 🧭 What is self-learning?

Self-learning means:
- Following your curiosity  
- Being willing to try (even when unsure)  
- Asking questions, reflecting, and practicing regularly  
- Learning not just to finish a task — but to understand how things work  

You don’t need to be fast. You just need to **stay in motion**.

> 🎯 Self-learning is not about having all the answers — it’s about **becoming the kind of person who seeks them**.

<br>

## 🧠 What to do when you feel stuck or doubtful

It’s normal to feel self-doubt — especially when learning something new.

When you feel like saying:
- “I’m not good at this” → say: _“I’m still learning this”_  
- “Others are ahead of me” → say: _“Everyone has a different starting point”_  
- “I’m stuck” → ask: _“What’s one small step I can take next?”_

Break the task into smaller pieces.  
Ask a question in Slack.  
Review your notes or try to explain the concept in your own words.

Remember: **the act of trying again is part of the learning.**

> 💬 “Every adversity, every failure, every heartache carries with it the seed of an equal or greater benefit.” — Napoleon Hill

## 🌱 The role of IKIGAI in learning

**IKIGAI** is a Japanese word that means “a reason for being.”  
It’s found where four things meet:

- What you love  
- What you’re good at  
- What the world needs  
- What you can be paid for

As a beginner, you don’t need to have all four — but self-learning helps you explore each area.

Learning new skills helps you:
- Discover what you enjoy  
- Build what you’re good at  
- Understand what’s useful to others  
- Eventually turn those skills into opportunities

> 📌 Self-learning is a path to **finding your IKIGAI**, one small skill at a time.

<br>

## 🛠️ When to ask for help (and why it’s a strength)

Asking for help is not a sign of weakness — it’s a skill.

Here’s when you should reach out:
- You’ve tried something and it still doesn’t make sense  
- You’re spending too long stuck and feel frustrated  
- You want to double-check your understanding  
- You want feedback or encouragement

Try this format when asking:
> “I’m working on ___ and I’m confused about ___. Here’s what I tried so far.”

This shows initiative — and makes it easier for mentors and peers to support you.

> 🙌 Self-learning doesn’t mean you have to do everything alone — it means you take the **first step** and ask when needed.

<br>

## 🔁 Final tips to stay on track

- Learn in small, focused chunks (25 minutes is enough)  
- Reflect often — ask: “What did I understand today?”  
- Stay curious — Google things, test ideas, take notes  
- Document your progress (README, notes, check-ins)  
- Encourage others — teaching is the best form of learning

> ✨ Self-learning is not a race. It’s a rhythm. Find yours and keep going.
