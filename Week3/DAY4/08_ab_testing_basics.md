# 🧪 What is A/B testing?

**A/B testing** is a simple way to test two ideas — to see **which one performs better**.

You:
1. Show **Version A** to one group
2. Show **Version B** to another
3. Measure what happens — and go with the winner

> It's like a science experiment for choices.

Instead of guessing what will work…  
You **test and let the data decide**.

Lina wants to attract more people to her lemonade stand.

She creates two signs:
- **Version A**: “Try our fresh lemonade – just €1.5!”
- **Version B**: “Best-seller: Lemon flavor – Refresh your day!”

She puts each sign near her stand **on alternating days**.

Then she tracks:
- How many people walk over  
- How many buy

After 2 weeks, she notices:
- **Version B led to 30% more visits and 20% more sales**

🎉 That’s a win!  
Instead of guessing, Lina tested — and got results she could trust.

## 🧠 Designing good comparisons

The key to a successful A/B test is **a fair setup**.

| Term           | What it means                                 | Example from Lina                         |
|----------------|-----------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------|
| Control        | The original version (your baseline)          | Sign A: Her usual “Fresh lemonade” sign    |
| Treatment      | The new idea you're testing                   | Sign B: “Best-seller: Lemon flavor”        |
| Metric         | What you measure to decide who wins           | Number of cups sold                        |
| Sample Split   | Who sees what version                         | Half of the days use Sign A, half Sign B   |

<br>

## 📱 Real-world examples

### Netflix 🎬  
Tests different thumbnails for a movie:
- Version A = sad expression  
- Version B = action scene  
→ Tracks which gets more clicks

### Spotify 🎵  
Tests new button colors or playlist order:
- Some users see blue, others see green  
→ Measures play-through rates

### Landing pages or signup forms 🖱️  
- Version A = Short form  
- Version B = Long form with testimonials  
→ Measures signup conversion

In each case, **only one thing changes** — everything else stays the same.

<br>

## 🧪 What makes a test “good”?

A/B testing is only powerful when you:
- 🎯 Change **one thing at a time**
- 🧪 Give both versions a **fair shot** (equal time, users, or traffic)
- 📊 Measure **the same metric** in both groups
- 🧠 Let the test run long enough to collect enough data

Otherwise, it’s just guesswork in disguise.

<br>

## 🤔 What A/B testing can’t do

It can’t:
- Predict why something worked  
- Replace thoughtful design or user understanding  
- Always give clear answers (some tests are inconclusive!)

So use it **as a decision tool**, not a magic solution.
