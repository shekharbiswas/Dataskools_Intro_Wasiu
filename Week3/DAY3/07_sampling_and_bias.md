# 🧪 What is sampling?

In the real world, we almost never collect **all the data**.

Instead, we collect a **sample** — a small part of the population — and use it to make **larger conclusions**.

> Think of sampling like **tasting a spoonful of soup**:  
> If the spoonful is representative, you can guess how the whole pot tastes.

But what if the spoonful has all the salt?

That’s what **bias** is.

<br>

## 🍋 Lina’s survey gone wrong

Lina wants to improve her lemonade stand. She hands out a feedback form…

- On **sunny Saturdays only**  
- To **loyal regulars**
- Near the stand (but not to people who walked away)

Her survey results say:
> “Everyone loves the mint flavor!”  
> “Nobody complains about prices!”

But her cousin says:
> “That can’t be true — I saw people walk away after checking the menu.”

📉 Lina has just made a mistake called **sampling bias**.

<br>

## 🚨 What is sampling bias?

**Sampling bias** happens when your sample **doesn’t represent** the full population.

| Type             | What it means                                 | Example                                              |
|------------------|------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------|
| Sampling bias    | Your sample is too narrow or unfair            | Asking only weekend customers                        |
| Selection bias   | People choose themselves to join (or not)      | Only super-fans fill out feedback forms              |
| Non-response bias| Some people ignore your survey                 | Quiet customers don’t reply, so negative views are missed |
| Confirmation bias| You ask in a way that supports your belief     | “Don’t you love our lemonade?” (vs neutral question) |

<br>

## 🎯 Why does it matter?

Bad samples = bad decisions. Period.

Lina might:
- Promote the wrong flavor  
- Raise prices based on biased praise  
- Think everything is fine while losing quiet customers

Even large companies get this wrong:
- Election polls miss voters in rural areas  
- Health surveys miss uninsured or undocumented people  
- Apps test only with iPhone users and forget Android

<br>

## 🧪 Try it: Biased vs fair sampling

### Scenario A:
> “Ask 5 people near your desk what snack is best.”

→ Result: Chips win!

### Scenario B:
> “Ask 5 random students across campus.”

→ Result: Chips, chocolate, fruit, and coffee all show up.

🧠 Reflection:  
Which result is more useful if you're planning a school snack bar?

<br>

## 🎲 What’s random sampling?

**Random sampling** means every person or item has an **equal chance** of being selected.

| Good Sample                        | Biased Sample                          |
|------------------------------------|----------------------------------------|
| 100 people randomly across all days | Only regulars on sunny weekends        |
| 10 students from every department   | Just your friends                      |
| Survey sent to all newsletter users| Just Instagram followers               |

Random = Fair.  
Biased = Misleading.

<br>

## 📊 Quick fixes to avoid bias

- Sample from **different times, locations, and people**
- Use **anonymous forms** to reduce peer pressure
- Keep questions **neutral and open-ended**
- Don’t just look at who answered — think about who didn’t

<br>

## 📘 Real-world applications

| Context         | Biased if…                                   | Better if…                                     |
|-----------------|-----------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------|
| Product surveys | Only fans respond                            | Send to all recent buyers                      |
| A/B testing     | Only frequent users see the test             | Test on new + returning users                  |
| Education data  | Only honors students fill the feedback form  | Ask across performance levels and classes      |
| Voting polls    | Only city areas are sampled                  | Include rural and hard-to-reach voters         |

## 🏁 Summary

Sampling helps us make decisions **without having all the data**.

But biased samples = bad decisions.

So next time you “taste the soup”:
- Make sure it’s a **good mix**  
- Be aware of **who’s included**  
- Ask: **“Who might I be leaving out?”**
