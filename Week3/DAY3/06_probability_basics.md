Probability is a way of **measuring uncertainty**.  
It tells us how likely something is to happen — but it **doesn't guarantee** it will.

> Think of it like this:  
> If something has a **1 in 2 chance**, that means if you tried it many times, about **half the time**, it should happen.

You’ve probably seen:
- 50% chance of rain 🌧️  
- 1 in 6 chance of rolling a 6 🎲  
- 10% chance of someone clicking a button 🖱️

But what does that **actually mean** in a world of randomness?

<br>

## 🧃 Lina: predicting lemonade sales

Lina wants to plan smarter.

She notices:
- If the weather is sunny, she usually sells **12+ cups**  
- If it rains, she sells **6 or fewer**  
- Weather apps say **“60% chance of sun”** tomorrow

She asks:  
> “Does that mean I *will* sell 12 cups? Or is it still uncertain?”

The answer: It’s **likely**, but not guaranteed.  
Probability is **a guide**, not a promise.

<br>

## 📉 Events, outcomes, and examples

Let’s break it down:

| Term       | What it means                             | Lina’s example                          |
|------------|--------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------|
| Event      | Something that may or may not happen       | "It rains today"                         |
| Outcome    | The result of a specific trial             | 15 cups sold on Saturday                 |
| Probability| A number between 0 and 1 (or 0% to 100%)   | 0.6 chance of a sunny day                |

<br>

## 🎯 Independent vs Dependent Events

| Concept            | What it means                                  | Example                                   |
|--------------------|--------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------|
| Independent        | One event doesn’t affect the other              | Coin flips: getting heads doesn’t change future flips  
| Dependent          | One event changes the chance of another         | Weather & sales: if it rains, Lina sells less  
| Mutually Exclusive | Two things can’t happen at once                 | Rain & no rain on same day = impossible   |

<br>

## 🧪 Quick demos to try

### Coin flip (Independent)
- Flip a coin 10 times
- Track heads and tails
- Ask: Did you get 5 heads? Why or why not?

🧠 Lesson: Probability gives us **long-term expectations**, not guarantees

<br>

### Lemonade demand (Dependent)
Lina checks the forecast:
- Sunny: high demand (12–15 cups)
- Cloudy: average (8–11 cups)
- Rainy: low (5–7 cups)

Ask students:
- If tomorrow is sunny, what’s a **reasonable prediction**?
- How would they prepare?

<br>

## 🧪 User behavior example (tech context)

You run a website. 10 out of 100 visitors click “Sign Up”.

- What’s the probability someone will sign up? → 10%  
- What happens if we redesign the button?

This shows how **past data gives us probabilities** — and how changing conditions affect outcomes.

## ✅ Self-check questions

<details>
<summary>🎲 What is probability?</summary>
A number that describes how likely something is to happen — between 0 (impossible) and 1 (certain).
</details>

<details>
<summary>📉 What’s the difference between independent and dependent events?</summary>
Independent events don’t affect each other. Dependent events do — like weather and lemonade sales.
</details>

<details>
<summary>🧠 What does a 70% chance of something really mean?</summary>
That in the long run, it will happen about 7 out of 10 times — but not always in the short run.
</details>

<details>
<summary>💡 Why is probability useful in business?</summary>
It helps people predict likely outcomes, plan inventory, prepare resources, and reduce risk.
</details>

<br>

## 🏁 Summary

Probability helps us:
- **Manage uncertainty** instead of ignoring it  
- **Model real-world events** like weather, sales, or clicks  
- Make better decisions when things aren’t guaranteed
