# ⚖️ What are data ethics and bias?

Data is powerful — but it can also be unfair, incomplete, or harmful **if not handled responsibly**.

**Data ethics** is about:
- Asking who benefits or gets harmed by a decision  
- Being transparent about how data is collected and used  
- Thinking beyond the numbers — and including the people behind them

**Bias in data** means:
- Certain people or groups are **overrepresented** or **left out**
- The results may reflect unfair patterns
- Your decisions could unintentionally reinforce inequality

---

## 🧬 Real-life examples of bias

| Situation                              | Bias risk                                                             |
|----------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Hiring model trained on past resumes   | May favor people from dominant groups or certain universities         |
| Lending algorithm based on ZIP codes   | May block low-income applicants, even if they’re responsible borrowers |
| Health data collected mostly from men  | May miss key symptoms in women or underrepresented communities        |

💡 Even good intentions can lead to bad outcomes **if the data is biased**.

<br>

## 🍋 Lina: bias in a customer survey

Lina wants feedback, so she puts out a quick survey. But:

- She only prints it on sunny weekends  
- Only hands it out to smiling customers  
- Only asks people who bought Lemon

At the end of the week, she sees this in the responses:

> “We LOVE Lemon! Please make more weekend flavors!”

Lina almost stops selling Ginger… but then her friend Mia says:
> “Wait — what about weekday customers or Mint fans?”

### 🧠 What went wrong?

- 📅 **Sampling bias**: Only collected data on weekends  
- 😊 **Selection bias**: Asked only happy customers  
- 🍋 **Confirmation bias**: Already believed Lemon was best, so she noticed feedback that matched

<br>

## 🚦 How to reduce bias like Lina

Lina decides to improve her survey:
- She runs it for **a full 7 days**, not just weekends  
- Asks **every customer**, not just happy ones  
- Includes **questions for all flavors**

She even adds:
> “Did you feel heard today?”  
> “What would you improve?”

💬 Her new data paints a **fuller picture** — and she keeps Ginger after learning it’s a weekday favorite.

<br>

## 💬 Data ethics in action

Even a lemonade stand can ask:
- Who am I collecting data from — and who am I missing?  
- Could this decision accidentally exclude someone?  
- How can I make the experience fairer?

This mindset is what makes **ethical analysts** — people who **don’t just find answers, but also ask better questions**.

<br>

## 📌 Common types of bias (with examples)

| Type of Bias       | What It Means                                  | Lina’s Example                                 |
|--------------------|--------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------|
| Sampling Bias      | Only collecting from a subset of the audience    | Asking only weekend buyers                     |
| Selection Bias     | Choosing who gets to give data                   | Only asking customers who look friendly        |
| Confirmation Bias  | Favoring data that supports your belief          | Believing Lemon is best, so only noticing that |
| Survivorship Bias  | Ignoring those who dropped out or didn’t respond | Not hearing from people who don’t return       |
| Reporting Bias     | People don’t answer honestly                     | Customers afraid to give negative feedback     |

## ✅ Self-check questions

<details>
<summary>⚖️ What are data ethics about?</summary>
Using data in a way that is fair, transparent, and doesn’t unintentionally harm or exclude people.
</details>

<details>
<summary>🚨 What is sampling bias?</summary>
It’s when you collect data from only part of your audience — and miss other important perspectives.
</details>

<details>
<summary>🧪 What is confirmation bias?</summary>
It’s when you look for or notice only the data that supports what you already believe — and ignore other information.
</details>

<details>
<summary>📅 What mistake did Lina make with her survey timing?</summary>
She only collected feedback on sunny weekends, missing input from weekday customers.
</details>

<details>
<summary>🙋 Why is it important to include unhappy or quiet users in feedback?</summary>
Because they might have valuable insights, and excluding them can lead to biased or incomplete decisions.
</details>

<details>
<summary>🧠 How can you design fairer data collection?</summary>
Ask a diverse range of people, spread it across time, and avoid leading or selective questions.
</details>
