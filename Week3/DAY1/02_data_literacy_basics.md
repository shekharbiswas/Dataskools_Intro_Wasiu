# 📖 What does it mean to be data literate?

Being **data literate** means you can:
- Understand what data is telling you
- Interpret charts, tables, and dashboards
- Ask the right questions when something looks wrong
- Spot misleading information or gaps in logic

🧠 It’s not about coding — it’s about **thinking clearly with numbers.**

## 💡 Why does data literacy matter?

In today’s world, we constantly see numbers:
- App dashboards
- Business reports
- Polls in the news
- Fitness trackers
- Social media stats

But most people don’t pause to ask:
> “Is this chart actually showing what I think it is?”  
> “How was this number calculated?”  
> “What’s missing from this graph?”

**That’s where YOU come in.**  
A data-literate person brings clarity — not just clicks.

<br>

## 🍋 Lina’s story: reading her lemonade dashboard

Lina uses a Google Sheet dashboard to track:

## 📘 8 essential terms every analyst should know

Let’s walk through some **core data terms** using Lina’s stand:

<br>

### 1. **Metric**
A number that tracks something.

**Example:**  
`Total cups sold = 89`, `Revenue = €142`

<br>

<img width="238" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/72bcc139-63a0-409e-ae90-6c1031832f99" />

<br>

### 2. **Trend**
A pattern over time — going up, down, or staying flat.

**Example:**  
Lemon sales trend upward every Saturday.

<br>

### 3. **Outlier**
A value far outside the normal range.

**Example:**  
One day Lina sold 30 Ginger cups — way more than usual. Why?

<br>

### 4. **Average (Mean)**
The sum of values divided by how many values there are.

**Example:**  
If Lina sold 10, 12, and 8 cups over 3 days, her average = (10+12+8)/3 = 10

<br>

### 5. **Median**
The middle value in a list when sorted.

**Example:**  
If Lina’s daily sales were [8, 9, 9, 10, 18], the median is **9**

<br>

### 6. **Proportion**
How much one part contributes to the whole.

**Example:**  
Mint made up 25% of total cups sold last week.

<br>

### 7. **Dashboard**
A visual layout showing important metrics and trends.

**Example:**  
Lina’s Google Sheet shows flavor sales, revenue by day, and cup count on one page.

<br>

### 8. **Misleading chart**  
A chart that hides or distorts the real message — often by **starting the y-axis too high** or **skipping important labels**.

## 🧠 Tips for reading data

- **Always check the time range**: Is this daily, weekly, or monthly data?
- **Look for labels**: What are the units? Cups? Euros?
- **Ask what’s missing**: Are we seeing all the flavors? Are any dates skipped?
- **Use your common sense**: If something feels off — dig deeper.

<br>

## 📊 Example from Lina’s dashboard

| Date       | Flavor | Cups | Price | Revenue |
|------------|--------|------|-------|---------|
| 2024-06-21 | Lemon  | 12   | 1.5   | €18.00  |
| 2024-06-21 | Mint   | 10   | 2.0   | €20.00  |
| 2024-06-21 | Ginger | 5    | 2.0   | €10.00  |

**At first glance:** Lemon sold the most  
**But:** Mint earned the most revenue!

A data-literate person sees both the **volume** and the **value**.
