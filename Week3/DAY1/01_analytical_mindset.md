
# 💭 What makes someone think like an analyst?

Before using fancy tools or complex models, great analysts start with something simple:  
👉 **A curious, structured way of thinking.**

This is what we call the **analytical mindset** — the ability to:
- Ask good questions
- Break problems down
- Think in terms of logic, evidence, and alternatives

It’s not about being perfect. It’s about being intentional.

<br>

## 🧠 The habits of an analyst

Here are key habits that analysts build over time:

| Habit                      | Description                                                                 |
|---------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 🧩 Ask structured questions | Move from “What’s happening?” to “Why is it happening?” and “What should we do?” |
| 🔍 Observe carefully        | Look for patterns, exceptions, trends, and small signals                    |
| ✏️ Write it down           | Turn ideas into hypotheses or tests — like little science experiments        |
| 📏 Measure and compare     | Use data to confirm or challenge what you think                            |
| 🧘 Stay calm with uncertainty | Don’t panic when there’s no clear answer — ask better follow-ups instead     |

<br>

## 🍋 Lina’s lemonade story: becoming an analyst

Lina is selling lemonade, but she’s also developing her **data brain** without realizing it.

> Week after week, she starts asking smarter questions.

At first, she just noticed sales go up or down.  
Then, she started asking things like:

- “Why did Lemon do so well last weekend?”  
- “Did the chalkboard actually help?”  
- “Should I keep Ginger if it barely sells?”  

Instead of just guessing, she starts:
- Filtering her data by date and flavor  
- Writing short notes when something changes  
- Comparing weather and sales volume  
- Setting up a small feedback form

🎯 This is how the **analytical mindset** begins: with simple patterns and real-life questions.

<br>

## 🔗 Structured questions = better decisions

Lina used to ask:
> “Should I stop selling Ginger?”

Now she reframes:
> “On which days does Ginger sell well, and why?”

Another example:
> ❌ "Mint isn’t working."  
> ✅ "Did the new recipe improve Mint sales on weekends compared to weekdays?"

📈 Analysts **reframe problems as testable questions** — and then gather data to explore them.

<br>

## 🧠 Your turn: Ask like an analyst

Try applying the mindset to these cases:

| Scenario                             | Weak question                      | Strong analyst-style question                               |
|--------------------------------------|------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------|
| You notice views dropped on a video | “Why did my views drop?”           | “What changed around the time views dropped — title, time, platform?” |
| A product isn’t selling              | “Why is nobody buying it?”         | “Which audience segments are engaging — and which aren't?”  |
| Feedback says “too expensive”        | “Should I lower the price?”        | “Which customers think it’s expensive — and what are their alternatives?” |
