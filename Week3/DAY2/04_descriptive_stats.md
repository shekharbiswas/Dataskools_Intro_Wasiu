# 📊 What are descriptive statistics?

Descriptive statistics help us **summarize and describe a dataset**.

Instead of staring at rows and rows of numbers, we look for:
- What's **typical** (mean, median, mode)
- How much **it varies** (range, standard deviation)
- How **uncertain** the results are (margin of error)

It’s like telling a story about your data — using **just the right highlights.**

<br>


## 🍋 Lina: how she uses stats

Lina runs a lemonade stand and wants to understand:
- What’s the **average number of cups** she sells per day?  
- Which flavor is most common?  
- How different are her daily earnings?  
- Was last Saturday’s spike just random — or a sign?

She uses **descriptive stats** to get answers.

<br>


## 🧮 Central tendency: mean, median, mode

| Term   | What it means                  | Lina's example                                |
|--------|--------------------------------|------------------------------------------------|
| Mean   | The average                    | 10 + 12 + 8 = 30 → Mean = 30 ÷ 3 = 10          |
| Median | The middle value (when sorted) | [8, 10, 12] → Median = 10                      |
| Mode   | The most frequent value        | If she sold 10 cups on 4 days out of 5 days → Mode = 10      |

💡 **Use mean** to describe general behavior.  
💡 **Use median** when data has outliers (e.g., one super high day).  
💡 **Use mode** to find what happens most often.

<br>


## 📏 Variability: range and standard deviation

It’s not enough to know the average — we also need to know **how much things change**.

| Term                | What it tells us                     | Example                            |
|---------------------|--------------------------------------|------------------------------------|
| Range               | Max – Min                            | 18 cups – 7 cups = 11              |
| Standard deviation  | How spread out the numbers are       | Higher SD = more daily ups/downs   |
| Margin of error     | Uncertainty in our average estimate  | Lina might say: "Average = 10 ± 2" |

🧠 Lina notices that on some days she sells **15+ cups**, and on others **barely 7**.  
That’s high variability — important for planning stock.

<br>

## ✅ Self-check questions

<details>
<summary>📊 What’s the difference between mean and median?</summary>
Mean is the average of all values. Median is the middle value when sorted — less affected by outliers.
</details>

<details>
<summary>📉 What does range tell you?</summary>
It shows how much your data spreads from lowest to highest — how variable it is.
</details>

<details>
<summary>🧠 What’s the use of knowing the “shape” of your data?</summary>
It helps you understand whether your data is balanced, skewed, or has extreme values.
</details>

<details>
<summary>📏 What is margin of error?</summary>
It shows how uncertain or variable your average is — often shown as ± a value.
</details>
